description: "When Ontario removed roughly 700 automated speed enforcement cameras from school and community safety zones in 2025, neighbourhoods lost one of their few sources of hard evidence about speeding. What happens when residents start collecting that evidence themselves?\r

  \r

  This talk introduces [CIVIC](https://ourcivicfutures.ca/) (Community-centred Infrastructures for Visible and Inclusive Cities), an urban living lab in London, Ontario. Residents host low-cost, privacy-by-design Telraam traffic sensors and noise monitors in their own windows. These sensors count cars, cyclists and pedestrians and estimate speeds, all without storing images. Professor Carrie Mitchell will walk through how the network was built, what the data shows, and how residents and city staff are using it to make the case for traffic calming. She’ll also cover what’s hard: data gaps, privacy trade-offs, and keeping a volunteer sensor network running. Finally, she’ll ask what it would take to scale community-owned street data across Ontario, and where civic technologists can help."
